The teaching · in your roles

What actually changed, and why

The Emcee · Hierarchy

One voice at a time

v1 gave every card the same volume: same cream box, same colored stripe on top. Six speakers, one mic, all talking at once.

v2 runs it like your run-of-show. Headlines get gradient ink and a spotlight shadow, body copy steps back to 72 percent brightness, and color only speaks when it has a line. The eye knows who has the mic.

The Host · Atmosphere

Light the room, don't paint it

v1 used color as paint: flat fills, one-direction gradients. That's daylight fluorescent. It's why it felt like a handout.

v2 uses color as light: a dark stage, floodlight glows in the corners, film grain so the dark feels like air, glass panels that blur what's behind them. It's the difference between a venue at 2 PM and the same venue at 8:55 PM before kickoff.

The Officiant · Timing

Motion with intent

v1 had hover lifts and nothing else. No entrance, no ceremony.

v2 staggers the hero: eyebrow, headline, lede, meta cards rise in sequence a tenth of a second apart, the way you pace a vow. Itinerary times got glowing timeline nodes. Unlocked badges shimmer. And it all respects reduced-motion settings, because a good officiant reads the room.

The Game Designer · Feedback

Juice the verbs

v1's bingo marks just changed color. The buttons were flat. You and Quinn have shipped games; you know that's unrewarded input.

v2 adds game feel: marked bingo cells stamp gold, tilt, and get a checkmark. Kick buttons glow on hover and compress on press. Tickets have punch-holes. Every action pays the player.

How to read my work from now on

Your four questions

When I hand you anything visual, audit me with these. They map to roles you already master, so you don't need design vocabulary to direct me precisely.

Ask 1

"Who has the mic?"

If you can't tell what to read first, hierarchy failed. Send it back.

Ask 2

"What time of day is this room?"

If the design has no lighting mood, it's a handout. Name the mood you want and I'll relight it.

Ask 3

"Where's the ceremony?"

If nothing enters, reveals, or lands, the page has no timing. Even one staged entrance changes everything.

Ask 4

"Did the click pay me?"

Every interactive element should reward the touch. You've known this since your first credit. Hold me to game-industry standards.
